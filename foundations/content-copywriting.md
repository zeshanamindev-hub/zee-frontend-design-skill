# Content & copywriting standard: engaging for people, accepted by search engines

How the site's **wording** is written: copy that keeps visitors reading and acting, and that meets the quality standards Google, Bing and other search engines reward. Always written **after** competitor analysis (`workflows/competitor-analysis.md`) through the content strategy (`workflows/content-strategy.md`).

## 1. What search engines accept (verified Nov 2026; re-check for changes)
**Google**
- Rewards **helpful, reliable, people-first content** that shows **E-E-A-T**: Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trust (trust matters most).
- Self-check with **Who / How / Why**: who created it (clear authorship and credentials), how it was created (including any automation, where readers would expect to know), and why: to help people, not mainly to rank.
- In Oct 2026 Google expanded this guidance with a definition of **main content**, criteria around **effort and originality**, and a warning that **fabricated author/creator profiles** signal low quality.
- **AI-assisted content is allowed** when it's helpful and original. **Scaled content abuse** (many pages made mainly to manipulate rankings, by AI or humans) is spam. So are keyword stuffing, hidden text, doorway pages and site reputation abuse.
- **AI Overviews / AI Mode** need no special formatting: llms.txt, "chunking" and AI-specific rewriting aren't needed (Google guide, May 15, 2026). Good SEO is the AI-search strategy.

**Bing (and Copilot)**
- Bing's Webmaster Guidelines (rewritten Feb 2026) cover Bing search, Copilot and grounding results. They define GEO as content eligibility for **grounding and citation** in AI answers. In practice: clear, focused, useful, original content, crawlable pages, structured data that matches visible content, fast pages.
- Use **IndexNow** to notify Bing (and other participating engines) of new or updated URLs; verify the site in Bing Webmaster Tools.

**Shared bottom line:** original, specific, accurate, well-structured content written for the reader, with honest authorship and evidence. No tricks.

## 2. Engagement writing: keep people reading and acting
| Principle | How to write it |
|---|---|
| **Lead with the outcome** | First line says what the reader gets: "Get a quote in 2 minutes", not "Welcome to our website" |
| **Answer first** | Start each section with the direct answer or key point, then explain (inverted pyramid) |
| **Customer language** | Reuse words from reviews and sales calls; cut internal jargon |
| **Specific > generic** | Numbers, names, places, timeframes, but **only real ones** |
| **Benefit → feature** | "Never miss a payment: automatic reminders 3 days before" |
| **Scannable** | Short paragraphs (1–3 sentences), meaningful subheads, bullets for lists, tables for comparisons, bold for key phrases (sparingly) |
| **Rhythm** | Mix short and medium sentences; average under ~20 words; active voice; "you" more than "we" |
| **Proof next to claims** | Every big claim is followed by evidence (testimonial, number, case study, certification) |
| **Handle objections** | Answer the top worries where they arise (price, time, risk, trust) |
| **One clear next step** | CTA = verb + outcome; microcopy removes risk ("No card needed") |
| **Story where it helps** | About pages, case studies, founder notes: real people, real problems, real results |
| **Tone fits the audience** | Use the voice & tone guide; consistent across pages and microcopy |

**Headline helpers** (thinking tools, not templates): outcome + audience ("Payroll for small teams, done in 5 minutes") · question the reader is asking · number + promise (only true numbers) · before → after. Also PAS, AIDA, Before–After–Bridge for structuring sections.

## 3. SEO wording rules (write for the topic, not the keyword)
- **Search intent first:** the page format must match what searchers want (guide, comparison, product, service, local page).
- **Primary query** appears naturally in: title tag, H1 (or a close variant), the first ~100 words, at least one H2, the URL slug, and an image alt where it truly describes the image. Never force it.
- **Cover the topic fully:** related subtopics, entities (products, places, standards, tools), synonyms and natural variations, the questions people ask. Semantic completeness beats repetition.
- **Headings as a table of contents:** H2s that state benefits or match real questions; logical H2 → H3 nesting.
- **Answer-first paragraphs** under question headings: a 1–2 sentence direct answer, then detail. This is for reader clarity, not a special "AI format".
- **Unique value:** add what top results lack: first-hand experience, original data, photos, local detail, worked examples, expert quotes.
- **Internal links** with descriptive anchors ("compare our pricing plans", not "click here") to related pages, both ways within topic clusters.
- **Freshness:** update facts, prices and dates; show "last updated" on content that changes.
- **Never:** keyword stuffing, repeated city-name lists, hidden text, near-duplicate location pages, spun/AI filler pages, fake reviews or authors.

**Title & meta formulas**
- Title (~50–60 characters, unique): `Primary keyword: benefit or differentiator | Brand` · local: `Service in City | Brand`
- Meta description (~140–160 characters, unique): what they get + proof + CTA. It doesn't affect ranking but drives clicks.
- Alt text: what the image shows and why it matters, under ~125 characters; empty `alt=""` for decorative images.
- URL slug: short, lowercase, hyphenated, the topic (`/services/roof-repair`).

## 4. Wording by page type
| Page | Structure & wording |
|---|---|
| **Homepage** | H1 = core value prop; who it's for; 3 key benefits with proof; how it works; social proof; one primary CTA repeated |
| **Service page** | H1 = service (+ location if local); problem it solves; what's included; process; pricing or price guidance; proof (case/reviews); FAQs from real questions; CTA |
| **Product page** | Benefit-led title + key specs; scannable features; usage/fit info; reviews; shipping/returns; FAQs |
| **Category/collection** | Short intro (who it's for, how to choose) above products; helpful buying guide below; unique text per category |
| **Local landing page** | Real local content: area served, local projects/photos, local reviews, address/map, local regulations or conditions. No city-swapped templates |
| **About** | Real story, team with real names/photos/credentials, values with evidence, milestones |
| **Pricing** | Plan names that describe the buyer; what's included in plain words; billing FAQ; guarantee |
| **Blog / guide** | Answer the query in the intro; scannable sections; original examples or data; author bio with real credentials; date; related links; CTA relevant to the topic |
| **FAQ** | Real questions in customer words; direct first sentence; link to deeper pages (FAQ rich results no longer show in Google, but FAQs still help users) |

## 5. E-E-A-T in the wording
- **Experience:** "We installed 340 solar systems in Lahore since 2019" (if true), with real project photos. First-hand details only someone who did it would know.
- **Expertise:** real author/expert names, credentials, and a link to their profile.
- **Authority:** real certifications, memberships, press, partners (with permission).
- **Trust:** accurate claims, sources for facts, transparent pricing and policies, contact details, real reviews, dates.
- **Never fabricate** authors, credentials, reviews, numbers or awards. Google explicitly flags fake creator profiles, and it's dishonest.

## 6. Readability
- Consumer sites: aim for roughly grade 7–9 reading level (Flesch Reading Ease ~60–70); expert B2B can go higher. Check with a readability tool (Hemingway, Yoast/Rank Math readability, or a script).
- Front-load key words in headings, links and bullets (people scan the first 2–3 words).
- Define jargon on first use; spell out acronyms once.
- Accessible wording: descriptive link text, clear error messages, no meaning carried only by color or icons.

## 7. AI-slop copy (banned) → write instead
| Banned | Instead |
|---|---|
| "Unlock the power of…", "Elevate your…", "Revolutionize…", "Seamless", "Cutting-edge", "Game-changer", "In today's fast-paced world", "Look no further", "Take it to the next level", "Delve into", "Harness", "Robust solution", "Empower" | A specific outcome, in the customer's words |
| Lorem ipsum, "Your text here" | Real copy or `PLACEHOLDER: <what's needed>` |
| Invented stats ("10,000+ happy clients"), fake testimonials | Real numbers and attributed quotes, or nothing |
| Three-adjective strings ("fast, secure and reliable") | One proven claim with evidence |
| Generic CTAs ("Submit", "Learn more" as the main action) | Verb + outcome ("Get my free estimate") |
| Same-structure sentences on every section | Varied rhythm; each section has its own job |

## 8. Content QA checklist (run before handover)
**Search-engine standards**
- [ ] Matches search intent; one primary intent per page; no cannibalization
- [ ] People-first: answers the query fully; adds original value beyond the top results
- [ ] E-E-A-T visible: real author/business info, credentials, evidence, dates
- [ ] No spam patterns: stuffing, doorway/near-duplicate pages, hidden text, scaled filler, fake authors/reviews
- [ ] Title, meta, H1, H2 outline, slug, alt texts, internal links, schema all present and accurate
- [ ] Facts checked; sources cited where needed; claims match what the business can prove
**Engagement**
- [ ] First screen states the outcome and the next step
- [ ] Scannable: subheads, short paragraphs, lists/tables
- [ ] Customer language; objections answered; proof next to claims
- [ ] CTA wording is specific and consistent
- [ ] No banned AI-slop phrases
**Readability & accessibility**
- [ ] Reading level fits the audience; jargon explained
- [ ] Descriptive links; clear microcopy and errors
**Local/multilingual (if relevant)**
- [ ] Local pages genuinely unique; NAP consistent; hreflang for languages

## 9. Localization
Write for the market (currency, units, dates, local proof, local terms). RTL languages (Urdu, Arabic): `dir="rtl"`, logical CSS properties, fonts with proper script support (typography.md §9). Use hreflang for language/region versions.

## 10. Deliverable per page
Messaging reference · page copy by section · title + meta · H1/H2 outline · slug · alt texts · internal links · CTA labels · microcopy · schema type · placeholders list.
