# 05 · UI/UX usability rules (H1–H14)

| ID | Rule | How to apply | Honest caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H1** | **Nielsen's 10 heuristics** (NN/g) | Visibility of status · match with real world · user control & freedom · consistency & standards · error prevention · recognition over recall · flexibility & efficiency · aesthetic & minimalist design · help users recognise, diagnose, recover from errors · help & documentation. Use them as the audit backbone (`../../workflows/design-audit.md`). | Heuristics find problems; they don't prioritise them. Severity rating is still a judgement. |
| **H2** | **Shneiderman's 8 golden rules** | Strive for consistency · universal usability (novice ↔ expert, shortcuts) · informative feedback · dialogs that yield closure · prevent errors · easy reversal · keep users in control · reduce short-term memory load. | Written for desktop software; still valid, but the wording predates touch and AI. |
| **H3** | **Norman's design principles** | Visibility · feedback · constraints · mapping · consistency · affordance (+ signifiers) · simplicity (conceptual model). | "Affordance" is often misused for "looks clickable". That's a *signifier* (I2). |
| **H4** | **Recognition over recall** | Show options instead of asking users to remember them: recent searches, visible labels on icons, autocomplete, inline examples ("e.g. 0300 1234567"). | Icon-only toolbars are recall; add labels or tooltips. |
| **H5** | **Consistency and standards** | Internal (same component = same behaviour) + external (platform conventions, U3). Keep a component inventory; one pattern per job. | Consistency with a *bad* existing pattern isn't a virtue in a redesign; change it everywhere at once. |
| **H6** | **Visibility of system status** | Timing ladder: < 1 s subtle state change · 1–10 s spinner/skeleton · > 10 s progress with estimate + cancel · > 60 s run in background + notify. Always show save state, upload %, filters applied, selected count. [Source: DAE-UX steering] | Over-notifying (toasts for everything) is noise. |
| **H7** | **User control and freedom** | Clear exits (close, cancel, back), undo (I5), edit before submit, dismissible dialogs (Escape), no forced flows. | "Freedom" without guidance can stall novices; offer a recommended path. |
| **H8** | **Error prevention** | Constraints (date pickers, masks), confirmations only for destructive and irreversible actions, inline validation **on blur** (not on every keystroke), disable impossible options with an explanation. | Confirmation dialogs everywhere train users to click "OK" blindly. |
| **H9** | **Progressive disclosure** | Show the essential 20% first; reveal advanced options on demand (accordion, "More options", step 2). Multi-step forms show "Step 2 of 4". | Hiding needed info costs clicks and trust; put critical info up front (price, fees). |
| **H10** | **Cognitive load reduction** | See U21. Chunk (U20), recognise (H4), defaults (I6), plain language (grade 6–8 reading level for consumer sites), one task per screen on mobile. | Over-simplification can remove expert value. |
| **H11** | **Feedback principle** | Every action gets a response within 100 ms: pressed state, inline success, toast for background results, error at the field. | Feedback must be perceivable by screen readers (`aria-live`, A9). |
| **H12** | **Affordance principle** | Controls look operable: buttons have a fill or clear boundary (≥ 3:1), links underlined in body text, draggable items have handles, inputs look like inputs (inset, per Kennedy's "light from the sky"). | Flat design weakened affordances; keep at least one strong cue per control. |
| **H13** | **Mapping principle** | Controls map to their effects spatially and conceptually: slider left→right = less→more, controls next to what they change, volume up = up. | Cultural direction matters (RTL layouts mirror horizontal mappings). |
| **H14** | **Accessibility-first principle** | Accessibility is designed in from the wireframe (contrast tokens, focus states, semantic structure, target sizes), not audited at the end. See the A-rules. | "Accessibility overlays" do not make a site conformant. |
